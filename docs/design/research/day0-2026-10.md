# Day 0 to Day 1: the first result (October 2026)

D18 settled *what* Day 0 is for: a warm-up that teaches the mechanic, then a first
real result the next day. It left the details open: which ten questions, what the
result may claim from ten answers, which predictions resolve by tomorrow, and what the
first real answer says. This round answers those, from a fresh run-through, a little
arithmetic, and the sources below.

It ran alongside D18's build (steps 97–103, 2026-10-08 and 09), so the run-through in
§1 shows the Warmup as it was before that build. §2, §3 and §6 still applied to the
built version and became steps 104–107; §5 and §7 were built in close to the form
proposed here (steps 99 and 101).

Tags as in [`market.md`](market.md): **[primary]** the experimenter's or company's own
report; **[secondary]** a summary or vendor blog; **[unverified]** a number found only
in secondary places.

So the gap D18 names is real and measurable: between the Warmup's verdict and the
first real feedback lie seven days, and the verdict it leaves behind is mostly noise
(§2).

## 2. What ten answers can say

The Warmup's headline comes from `classifyDirection`: a lean is named when accuracy
differs from mean confidence by more than 5 points. With ten two-choice answers,
accuracy moves in steps of 10 points, so for most stated confidences *any* result
but one exact count is a lean.

Binomial arithmetic for a person who is **perfectly calibrated** (stated confidence
equals their true hit rate), ten answers at one confidence:

| Says | Called a lean under today's ±5 rule | Called a lean only outside the central 80% chance range | A person truly 15 points hot, called hot (80% range) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 70% | **73%** of the time | 8% | 26% |
| 75% | 47% | 13% | 37% |
| 80% | **70%** | 3% | 25% |
| 85% | 38% | 5% | 35% |
| 90% | 61% | 7% | 47% |

(Computed 2026-10-09 with the binomial at n = 10; a real Warmup mixes confidences,
which makes the spread slightly narrower but doesn't change the picture.)

Two things follow:

1. **Today's verdict is mostly luck for a calibrated person.** Seven in ten people
   who say 70% and mean it are told they run hot or cold. That's the "number built on
   noise" `CLAUDE.md` rules out, with an identity on top.
2. **Ten questions can't reliably find a real lean either.** Someone 15 points
   overconfident is caught a quarter to a half of the time. So no rule makes the
   ten-question verdict trustworthy; the honest result is the counts, with a lean
   named only when it's beyond what luck commonly does, and said as being about
   *these ten*.

The engine already has the tool: `chanceRange` (D4) gives the central share of
binomial outcomes at a given n and p. The chart uses the central half; for a
headline sentence the central **80%** is the right bar, because a sentence is read as
a conclusion and a grey bar is read as a range.

## 3. Which ten

D18 (1) says to draw the ten from the practice tables. One detail matters:

- The practice deck only pairs entries within a **closeness band** (`MAX_GAP`:
  12° of latitude, a fourfold area, 100 years…), because whole-table pairs were "about
  half giveaways" and a daily practice that's mostly obvious isn't worth opening
  (`retention-2026-10.md` §2.5).
- That band makes the set harder than the reference class, and harder sets show
  overconfidence (the hard–easy effect; Lichtenstein & Fischhoff 1977, and Juslin's
  account of it as partly a selection artefact). The practice says this and labels
  itself practice.
- The representative-sampling result D18 rests on (Gigerenzer, Hoffrage &
  Kleinbölting 1991; Juslin 1994) comes from pairs drawn from the **whole** class, easy
  ones included. A Warmup drawn from the band would rebuild a milder version of the
  tilt D14 removed.
- For a one-off Warmup the giveaways are a feature: a question you're sure of is where
  100% is the right answer, and the Warmup is the only place the app teaches the top
  of the scale.

**Proposed:** draw the ten without `MAX_GAP` (only `MIN_GAP`, so no answer rests on a
source discrepancy, and a longitude cap, so "east" has one answer).

**What was built instead (step 106).** The whole-class draw, tried on the fixed ten,
came out eight giveaways in ten (Jupiter or Neptune, Ben Nevis or Aconcagua, Venus
or Enceladus): representative, and no use as a one-minute lesson. So the ten are two
draws of five, one inside the band and one from the whole class, which spans the
scale from close calls to sure things while each half stays a random draw. It stays
the same ten for everyone (step 97 re-derives the answer key from the draw, so a
per-install seed would need the questions stored). The daily practice keeps its band.

  retention mechanism". A figure of about 90% of trial starts on Day 0 circulates
  but we found no primary source for it. **[unverified]**
  ([RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md))

Calibrate already asks for nothing before the Warmup: no account, no paywall,
notification permission only in context (step 38). Keep it. The RevenueCat
pattern (most trials start on Day 0) is a reason someone might want a Day-0 paywall;
`CLAUDE.md`'s position that Plus only becomes legible once predictions resolve still
holds, and GROWTH §7's trial gate is where that gets tested, not here.

## 5. A first real result on Day 1

- **The overconfidence a goal app should catch is about plans** (planning fallacy:
  Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, predicted 33.9 days, took 55.5). Trivia transfers
  weakly (Lichtenstein & Fischhoff 1980). Both already behind D18.
- **Near-future plans are concrete.** Construal-level work on task predictions
  (Peetz, Buehler & Wilson 2010, JESP 46; a Waterloo dissertation on unpacking)
  suggests predictions about the near future draw on specifics and are less
  optimistic. We could read only citations and an abstract, so this is direction,
  not a number. **[unverified]** That's fine: the argument for *tomorrow* doesn't
  rest on accuracy, it rests on time to first feedback.
- **Feedback that teaches is said-against-happened** (Benson & Önkal 1992;
  `confidence-2026-10.md` §6), and none arrives until something resolves. A due date
  of tomorrow at 19:00 (D9) puts the first one inside the first 30 hours.

**Starter ideas for tomorrow.** One per category, each answerable by the next
evening, worded so the person can make it theirs, and mixing the kind of thing
people expect to happen with the kind they half-expect not to (the range coverage
caveat in `CLAUDE.md`):

| Category | Starter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Work | I'll finish the one task I most want done by tomorrow evening | A plan: where the planning fallacy lives |
| Health | I'll be asleep by 11 tonight | Resolves overnight; most people's honest number is well under 90 |
| Finance | I won't buy anything I didn't plan to tomorrow | A real coin-flip for many, so low-to-middle numbers |
| Social | A friend I message today replies the same day | Kept from the current set (step 94 already sets it to Tomorrow) |
| Personal | I'll spend 20 minutes on something I keep putting off, by tomorrow night | Another plan; small enough to be checkable |

Step 99 built a set in the same spirit ("I'll finish my most important task
tomorrow", "I'll get outside for a walk tomorrow", "I won't buy anything unplanned
tomorrow", the friend's reply, "I'll read before bed tomorrow night"). None sets a
confidence (§7.12). The due-date preset on the first prediction becomes
**Tomorrow**; from the second prediction on, it goes back to **In a week**, because
the app's per-horizon analysis (A6) needs a mix and most real predictions aren't
about tomorrow.

## 6. What the first real answer says

The first resolution is the app's real "aha", and today it gets the ordinary line:
"In your 60–80% range, 1 of 1 has happened." Two risks at n = 1:

- **Outcome bias.** People judge a call by how it turned out (Baron & Hershey 1988).
  A first "it happened" reads as *I was right*, a first "it didn't" as *I was wrong*,
  and neither is what a 70% means.
- **Yes and No must stay identical** (rule 0.4). Any first-answer line has to be the
  same sentence either way.

So the first answer gets one teaching line instead of the bucket line, the same for
Yes and No, built from the stated number: **"That's your first. A 70% call should
come true about 7 times in 10, so one answer can't say much; 20 can."** (built as
step 107; the ends of the scale read "should always come true" at 100% and "nearly
every time" at 95–99%). It uses
natural frequencies (Gigerenzer & Hoffrage 1995, already the house style) and points
at the threshold without a countdown. From the second answer on, the bucket line
returns.

## 7. The Day-0 card

D18 (5): the counts as an invitation. With §2, the card can't carry a lean unless the
result did ("I run true" is as much luck as "I run hot"), and like today it carries
no 0–100 score. Proposed, on the
existing 360 pt canvas:

> **78% sure. 6 of 10 right.**
> Ten questions, one number each.
> How sure are you? · Calibrate

"Tricky" leaves the card (the questions no longer are), and the accessibility summary
reads the same two counts. Built as step 101 with slightly different words: "5 of 10
right" · "I was 77% sure. How sure are you?"

## 8. What to measure

GROWTH §7 already reads `warmup_completed / warmup_started` and a Day-0 first log
either side of D18. Two more would show whether the change did its job:

- **First real result within 48 hours of the first launch**, as a share of Warmup
  finishers: the thing D18 exists to move. Today it is near zero by construction.
- **Day-1 and day-7 return** for the same cohort (the same signed-in blind spot as the
  other Day-0 rows applies).
