# Eleventh round: people who come from other goal apps

## Focus

Ten rounds have cast people by ability, language, stage and mindset. None has cast them
by **the app they use now**, though most people who find Fairly Sure through "habit" and
"goal" will have one, and will bring its idea of what logging, a miss, a streak and a
price are. This round casts eight people from eight different places (a goal planner, a
habit tracker, a gamified self-care app, Apple's built-ins, nothing at all, a weight-loss
programme, an AI coach, a money-on-the-line commitment device) and tests the research's
eight hypotheses (`competitors-2026-11.md` §6):

| | Hypothesis | Runs |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Goal-setters put 90–100% on their own goals as commitment, not chance | all; 1, 6, 8 most |
| H2 | A habit-tracker user logs and closes a goal on one day and gets "not scored" | 2 |
| H3 | A goal planner's months-out dates leave nothing to come back for this week | 1 |
| H4 | Partial progress gets forced into Yes or into Can't tell | 1, 2 |
| H5 | A miss with a good excuse gets parked as Can't tell | 8, 6 |
| H6 | The Welcome back card reads as an invitation to someone who left after a bad result | 6 |
| H7 | Nobody from a capped app notices nothing is capped; trial copy trusted or not | 1, 7, 8 |
| H8 | Someone living in Fitness rings and Reminders won't log by hand without a widget or Siri | 4 |

## What's different this round (the criteria)

Kept from the method (profile §10): goals not button names, think-aloud "expected / got",
ease 1–7 per task, severity 0–4, an interruption, three comprehension questions from
memory. **New**, the rival lens, in every brief (block B below):

1. **The job, stated first.** Before the first tap, the persona writes what they hope the
   app does for them and what their current app does. The run is judged against that job
   (yes / partly / no), not only against our tasks.
2. **The store text as the only briefing.** Every persona gets the listing's actual
   subtitle, promotional text and opening description (block A), so the gap between what
   the listing promises and what the first minutes give is measured.
3. **"Reached for".** Each time the persona looks for something their app has (a
   recurring habit, progress, a pet, a widget, revising a number, friends, an excuse), it
   is logged with what they found. Tallied across runs, it is a feature-gap list with the
   rival that has each.
4. **The confidence probe.** Before saving any of their own goals they say the number and
   why. Commitment reasons ("if I say less I'm planning to fail") versus chance reasons
   ("most weeks I manage it") test H1.
5. **The rival verdict.** Switch, use both, or stay; the sentence they'd text a friend;
   and, having looked at Plus (never bought), whether they'd pay set against what they pay
   now. That last one feeds the open price decision (P1).

## Common blocks (paste into every brief)

**A. What you know** (the App Store page, word for word; nothing else):

> **Fairly Sure** · Know when to trust your gut · Productivity · Free, offers in-app purchases
>
> Log a prediction, say how sure you are, and find out if you were right to be. Sharp in
> health, shaky with money? Your calibration score shows you where.
>
> Most people are overconfident about some things and underconfident about others, and
> never find out which. Fairly Sure shows you.
>
> HOW IT WORKS. Write a prediction ("I'll finish the report by Friday"), set how confident
> you are, from 0 to 100%, and pick a due date. As you set it, you see how your past calls
> at that confidence turned out. When it comes due, Fairly Sure reminds you. Tap yes or
> no. That's it.
>
> YOUR RESULT IN 60 SECONDS. Don't want to wait weeks? The warmup quiz gives you a first
> read on day one: ten quick either-or questions, each with how sure you are, and how
> your confidence compared with how many you got right.

**B. Against the app you use now** (replace `<my app>`):

1. Before your first tap, write two lines: what you hope this app will do for you, in your
   own words, and what `<my app>` does for you today.
2. Whenever you look for something `<my app>` has, write "Reached for: <the thing>.
   Found: <what you found, or nothing>."
3. Each time you set a number on one of your own goals or plans, say the number and why
   you picked it, before you save.
4. After "Would I come back tomorrow?" in your report, add:
   ```
   Against <my app>
   - The job I came for: <my words> — did it do it? yes / partly / no
   - Switch / use both / stay with <my app> — <why, one line>

   - What I'd text a friend about it: "<one sentence>"
   ```
