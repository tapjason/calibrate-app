## What's different this round

**The combination: matched pairs.** Rounds 20–22 drew most of the cast at random and filled
the gaps, so when two runs disagreed, a dozen things differed between them. This round casts
**four pairs**. Each pair shares a start state and a situation and differs mainly on **one
axis**, so a split within a pair points at that axis:

| Pair | Shared | The contrast | Runs |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | A fresh install, from a friend's card | tech literacy: L1 against L5 | 1 Vusi, 2 Sander |
| B | Five months in (`long-time`), a card to make | culture: bragging frowned on against bragging welcome | 3 Ayu, 4 Noa |
| C | Back after weeks away (`return`) as a parent of a baby, the app killed mid-run | mindset and literacy: anxious L3 against curious L5 (the wildcard) | 5 Isabela, 6 Zainab |
| D | The first week on a shared phone, words to hide, a number to fix | age: 15 against 66 | 7 Kalani, 8 Prakash |

The wildcard is drawn into pair C's second seat: every axis at random, holding only the pair's
start state and a mindset other than anxious.

**The criteria: block B2, besides block B.** Block B stays word for word, so the eight pairs, the
words and the disappointment answer compare with rounds 20–22. Four measures are added, each
chosen to show something the last rounds couldn't:
1. **Sure before, managed after** (expectation against experience, after Albert and Dixon,
   2003). Before each task the tester says how sure, 0–100%, they are to manage it alone, and
   after, whether they did. A task expected easy and found hard is a broken promise and ranks
   first; one expected hard and found easy is something to show earlier. It suits this app: the
   testers' own calibration about the app.
2. **Taps to the first thing worth having**, and per task. It counts the effort that rounds
   20–22 only rated.
3. **Do I believe it?** 1–7, the first time the app says something about the tester. Credibility
   (factor 15) was read from words only.
4. **Thinking and frustration**, 1–7 at the end (two NASA-TLX items). Ease said "easy" while
   the walls of text dipped the mood; load separates "easy to do" from "hard to take in".

Also new: **two start states** (`first-week-school`, `first-week-retired`, added in this round's
commit), so pair D's teenager and retiree meet a week like their own, not an office runner's
(round 22's harness note on runs 4 and 6).

`.playwright-mcp/playtest-2026-11-14/run<n>-<moment>.png`. Then B2:

> **B2. Four more things this time.**
> 1. **Before each task,** write how sure you are, from 0 to 100%, that you'll manage it on
>    your own without a wrong turn. After it, write whether you did: alone, after a wrong turn,
>    or gave up.
> 2. **Count your taps** from the very first screen to the first thing you'd call worth having
>    (whatever that is for you), and roughly how many each task took.
> 3. **Do I believe it?** The first time the app tells you something about yourself (a result, a
>    score, a label), rate from 1 to 7 how much you believe it (1 = not at all, 7 = completely)
>    and say why.
> 4. **At the end, two more numbers** from 1 to 7: how much thinking it took (1 = hardly any, 7
>    = a lot), and how frustrated you felt (1 = not at all, 7 = very).
>

## Success criteria (synthesis only, never in a brief)

As round 22, plus:
- **Pair A:** both reach the Warm-up's result and can say what their friend's card means after
  the friend fold; Vusi doesn't leave before the result; a 69-year-old L1 and an L5 developer
  agree on what the number means, whatever they feel about it.
- **Pair B:** Ayu makes a card she'd show her trainer, leaving off what she likes (all of it, if
  she wants) and it says so; Noa makes one she'd post. Where the pair parts ways is the card's
  culture finding.
- **Pair C:** after the kill, both resume the run where they were, with the count whole; neither
  reads the pile as a scolding; Zainab reaches "Your rating" by headings.
- **Pair D:** both fix a number set minutes ago in a few taps, keeping words they changed; both
  find "Hide the words"; a category they named reads "Your own category", a built-in with only
  an emoji keeps its word; Prakash can say what still shows on a hidden card.
- **Sure before, managed after:** any task at 70% or more beforehand and failed or done after a
  wrong turn in two or more runs is a P1 finding.
- **Load:** frustration 5 or more in any run is read against that run's mood line.
- **Belief:** under 4 at any first verdict is read against what it said.
