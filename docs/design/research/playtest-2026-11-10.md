# Blind playtest, 2026-11-10: people who come from other goal apps

Raw findings, 2026-11-10. Eight blind runs on the web build, all by personas
never cast before, cast by **the app they use now** from the competitor
research (`competitors-2026-11.md`) and run to the plan in
`round11-plan-2026-11-10.md`. The build is step 166 (`e281c2d`); nothing in
the app changed during the round. These are what the testers saw, checked
against the code; they are not decisions. Where they differ, `CLAUDE.md`,
`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` and `UI_ROADMAP.md` win.

## Method

Each blind run was a fresh agent with only the Playwright browser tools, no
repo and no docs, given a persona, the App Store text word for word as all it
knew, goals (never button or screen names), an interruption, three
end-of-run questions and the fixed report format (thinking aloud with
"expected / got", ease 1–7 per task, severity 0–4 per issue), one after
another in one browser. New this round, the rival lens (plan, block B): the
job stated before the first tap, "Reached for" logged whenever the persona
looked for something their own app has, the reason given for every number set
on their own goals, and a closing verdict against their own app (switch, both
or stay; the friend sentence; Plus set against what they pay now).

Before them, `web-playtester` ran the plan's nine checks (a five-month due
date, Answer it now on the day of logging, Change answer within 24 hours, the
Can't tell count, Arabic / Polish / te reo Māori titles at 320 and 440, the
Welcome back card after 36 days, the answer that unlocks the rating, the chart
without colour, the console): all passed with no app errors. Its notes are
findings 20, 27 and 28. Screenshots are in `.playwright-mcp/playtest-2026-11-10/`
(gitignored).

**The cast** (plan, "The cast"): nobody from the last two rounds; eight
different "uses now"; fresh installs 1 and 4; returning or long-time 6, 7, 8;
non-native, plain-language or dyslexic 2, 4, 5, 6, 7; access needs 2 (one
thumb), 3 (low energy), 5 (dyslexia), 7 (Reduce Motion), 8 (tritanopia);
"7 in 10" 2 and 5; under 26 one, over 60 one; two old phones at 320, one
offline, one on slow data; the edge of the audience is 6 (body weight on a
weight-loss programme).

## What held up

- **The plain-count lines are the product.** "Your 60–80% calls in health: 14
  of 27 happened" under the slider, "You expected 2 or 3" after a run, "That's
  40%, against the 67% you said" after an answer. Every tester's first moment
  worth it was one of these, and two lowered a number because of one.
- **After a miss, nobody felt judged.** "Recorded: it didn't happen", the same
  for both answers, "one that doesn't isn't a mistake" (3: "I breathed out"),
  "Your rating moved from 89 to 88. One answer nudges it either way" (6).
  Nothing commented on weight or diet (6, the edge persona), and recap titles
  are off by default (6, 7).
- **Welcome back reads as an invitation** (6: "kinder than I expected"; H6).
- **Every number reconciled** across Resolve, Insights, the chart, History's
  filter, the categories and the Brier base rate, before and after a
  correction (8: "every number agreed with every other number").
- **It works offline** (4): the Warm-up, Log, Edit and practice, with no
  spinner and nothing asking for a connection; the interruption lost nothing.
- **The rating unlocked where she was** (5): the 20th answer's Resolve said so,
  with the number and its first read of her 60–80% range.
- **Fixing things is fast:** Change answer in two taps with a stated deadline
  (2, 6, 7, 8), Edit for category and date (1, 3, 4), delete with a clear
  confirmation (5).
- **Other scripts and Reduce Motion:** Arabic right to left and whole on every
  screen (7, web check), Polish and macrons whole at 320 (2, 3, web check);
  nothing moved under Reduce Motion, checked by script (7).
- **Plus is trusted where it's seen:** the trial, the reminder two days
  before, the 24-hour notice and "your score, curve, badges and cards stay
  free" were read and believed (1, 3, 4, 6, 8; H7).

## Ranked findings

"No decision" means the fix doesn't change what a screen says or how it's
arranged; "needs the owner" means it does, or touches a decided D number, or is
scope. Each finding names the runs that hit it and the axis that exposed it.
"Again" marks a finding from `playtest-2026-11-09b.md` that this cast hit too.

### P1: what loses people

1. **A daily habit is "not scored" every day, and the app says so three times
   per tick** (2; habit tracker, 61+, one thumb; H2). A walk logged in the
   morning as "I walk 30 minutes today" sits under "Next 7 days, due Wed"
   (the earliest due date is tomorrow, D41), so ticking it takes five taps
   through Answer it now, a warning ("You logged it today, though, so an
   answer today isn't scored"), Resolve's line and the after-answer line. He
   understood the rule, and kept reading it as "my walk doesn't count". It is
   the right rule (D41 closed a badge-in-three-minutes hole); the question is
   how often it's said, and whether the first same-day answer should be where
   the app teaches "log it the evening before". Repeating the habit then needs
   retyping: "Log it again" is offered only on Resolve right after an answer,
   not on History's details or Today, and a typed title that matches past
   ones shows their record (D32) but fills neither the category nor the due
   date. *Needs the owner* (D41 copy, D32, `logAgain`).
2. **"In a week" catches people who mean tomorrow** (2, 3, 4, 5, 6; five
   runs, the most-hit finding; again, finding 15). After the first prediction,
   Log's due date starts on In a week (D18, by design). Piotr nearly saved his
   daily walk for next Tuesday, Kiri for next week (at 320 the chips sit under
   the sticky Save bar until a scroll), Jonas saved his Sunday race for
   Tuesday, Valentina's "tomorow" in the title didn't move it, and Carol had to
   change it. *Needs the owner* (D18): Tomorrow as the default, or the last
   one used, or the date said in the Save button ("Save, due Tue, Nov 17").
3. **Long goals give nothing back, and progress has nowhere to go** (1; goal
   planner, first five minutes; H3, H4). Four yearly goals put "Your first
   answer: Mon, Apr 12, 2027" on Today. She found a weekly checkpoint herself;
   the app never suggests one for a date months out. Progress ("at 9 of 24")
   went into the title, where an edit makes it a new prediction from today
   (D45). *Needs the owner:* a line on Log when the due date is more than a few
   weeks out ("Far off. A nearer checkpoint gives you something back sooner.");
   whether a note that isn't the title belongs on a prediction.
4. **What other people can see, the third round running** (6; edge of the
   audience, coworkers near; again, finding 3, after Rahel and Marcus).
   Weigh-in titles with her weight sit in large type on Today and in the
   80–100% list; the due-day reminder's body is the title
