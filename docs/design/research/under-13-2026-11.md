# The under-13 rule (D70): design and build spec

2026-11-19. `docs/SHIP_READINESS.md` decision 2. The owner delegated the call to the main
session ("apply your recommendations"); the design analysis below was done the same day
(read-only, sources at the end). **Not legal advice**: the points under "For counsel" are
the owner's to take to a lawyer before the public US release.

**Confirmed by the owner 2026-11-19** ("Use all your recommendations"), and **amended the
same day by the D70/D71 review** (four reviewers: two code, record, policy; no blocker).
Where the review changed a call, the call below says what it was and what it is now, and
the build spec describes the code as it stands after the review's fixes, those of its
re-review's round two included (marked "round two"). What the rule still lets through is
listed under "Residuals".

## The decision

When Apple's Declared Age Range says under 13, the app is **local-only**: no sign-in, no
sync, no usage counts, no Coach, no RevenueCat, and Plus isn't offered. Everything else
works as usual. One predicate decides it, one store field carries it, and every upload path
reads that field.

Why: the app already asks the question (the Texas, Utah and Louisiana app-store acts tell
developers to use the store's signal, and Apple says "you must check the age" where
required). Holding a signal that someone is a child while still collecting from them is the
worst position under COPPA's "actual knowledge" standard. Data that "is stored on the device
and is never transmitted" is not collection (FTC COPPA FAQ F.5), so local-only removes the
question. Guideline 5.1.4(a) asks that an app stay useful "regardless of a person's age",
which the free core loop is.

Not adopted: keeping purchases (a third party would get a per-install id for a known child,
and Plus without the Coach is thin); removing the question (leaves the state acts' "verify"
duty unmet); keeping the id-less guest counts ("stays on this phone" would stop being
plainly true); blocking the app (5.1.4(a)).

## The main session's calls (2026-11-19), each for the owner or counsel to overturn

1. **A band counts as under 13 whenever it can include someone under 13** (reversed by the
   D70/D71 review). `isUnder13Band(b)` is true when the upper bound is present and the
   lower bound is absent or below 13. True for (null, 12), (null, 11), (9, 12), (null, 13),
   (12, 13), (null, 15), (null, 17), (12, 15). False for (13, 15), (13, 16), (16, 17),
   (18, null) and no band. **What it was:** the first call also required the upper bound to
   be 13 or less, so (null, 15) and (null, 17) read as not under 13. **Why it changed:**
   Apple says the gates a region uses "may differ from the age gates you specify", so a
   region whose first gate is 14, 15 or 17 can answer a child with (null, 15) or (12, 15),
   and the first predicate let that child through. The rule fails closed. **The cost:** in
   a region whose gates don't include 13, people aged 13 to 15 (or to 17) are local-only
   too. That side of the trade is point 8 for counsel. The predicate still takes both
   readings of Apple's bounds (the sandbox table's (nil, 12), inclusive, and the
   `upperBound` reference page, which reads as if it would be 13); the owner's sandbox
   check confirms which is real, and the rule fires either way. Malformed input, nothing
   the app keeps, counts as under 13 unless its lower bound reads 18 or over
   (`src/engine/ageBand.ts`, the header).
2. **Nothing that makes a record waits out Apple's answer and then goes on** (tightened by
   the D70/D71 review). On a launch that waits for the answer (iOS 26, with no stored band,
   a stored under-13 band, or a stored band that can't be read):
   - **RevenueCat is never set up before the answer, with no time limit.** The paywall
     waits at most 5 s, then shows its "couldn't load plans" state ("Plus plans couldn't
     load. Check your connection, then open this again."), and works once the answer is
     in.
   - **A sign-in waits up to 5 s and is then refused** with "Sign-in isn't ready yet. Try
     again in a moment." (`AGE_PENDING_SIGN_IN`); the same tap goes on once the answer is
     in.
   - **The guest flush and the rating prompt wait**, and since the re-review's round two
     so do the Coach, Refine (dormant) and the trial-ending reminder's RevenueCat read
     (`uploadsMayStart()`). Queueing a usage event doesn't wait: it sends nothing, and the
     queue is cleared if the rule comes on.
   - **A sync still waits at most 60 s and then goes on** (unchanged: a backup that never
     runs costs an adult their data if the phone is lost, and every gate still holds
     after the cap). Sign-out's last push waits at most 5 s, then sends only if the rule
     doesn't apply.

   **What it was:** a paywall open or a sign-in waited at most 5 s for the answer and then
   went on as before, on the argument that Apple's sheet is modal. A cold-start deep link
   to the paywall starts that wait before the sheet has been read, and an account made in
   that window could only be frozen afterwards, against the policy's promise that nothing
   is created under 13. Everywhere the answer isn't waited for (web, Jest, Android, iOS
   before 26, a stored band of 13 or over) nothing waits and nothing changes.
3. **An account already signed in is frozen, not signed out and not deleted.** Uploads and
   the session's refresh timer stop; the rows stay on the phone; Sign out and Delete account
   still work (Delete account is the one upload path left open: it removes data). The
   account may be an adult's on a child's phone, and deleting someone's data is the
   owner's call. Stopping the timer doesn't stop every renewal: see "Residuals".
4. **Erase and account deletion still clear the band**, as promised. The gate stays on in
   memory for the rest of that run, and the next launch asks Apple again before anything can
   be sent.
5. **Apple is asked at every launch where the API can run, whatever band is stored**
   (widened by the D70/D71 review). With a stored band of 13 or over it is asked in the
   background and nothing waits; on a return to the foreground in a long run it is asked
   again once 24 hours have passed since the last ask, while the rule applies or a band of
   13 or over is known. An under-13 answer closes the gate mid-run and stops a sync or a
   flush in flight before its next request; a different band of 13 or over is saved; a
   decline, "not available" or an error keeps the stored band. **What it was:** only a
   stored under-13 band was asked about again; any other stored band was never asked
   again. **Why it changed:** the band belongs to the install, not to the Apple Account,
   so a phone handed to a child by switching accounts, or an app restored from a backup,
   kept the adult's band. **The consequence:** an adult whose iPhone is set to ask first
   sees Apple's sheet again on the anniversary of their answer. Whether every other repeat
   is silent is for the iPhone to show (below).
6. **The rating prompt isn't requested** on a local-only phone, nor while Apple's answer
   is pending (the moment is kept for after it).
7. **No band means today's behaviour**: declined, unavailable, older iOS, web, Jest. **A
   stored band that can't be read is not "no band"** (the D70/D71 review): a read that
   throws, or a row that isn't a valid band, is "unknown", and the gate stays shut for the
   run unless Apple then answers 13 or over (`readStoredBand`, `closeGateForUnknownBand`,
   the store's `loadUnknown`). Refined by the re-review's round two: a read that throws is
   tried once more before it counts (a busy database shouldn't close the gate for a whole
   run); where Apple's API can never have run (web, Android, iOS before 26) a read that
   throws is "no band", since none can be stored there; a row that isn't a valid band is
   still unknown everywhere.

## API facts (Apple, `expo-age-range` 0.2.21)

- Gates 13 / 16 / 18. Apple's sandbox table ("age ranges are inclusive"): under 13 is
  (nil, 12); 13–15 is (13, 15); 16–17 is (16, 17); 18+ is (18, nil). Regulated regions can
  replace the gates: Apple says the gates a region uses "may differ from the age gates you
  specify". So a lower bound can appear on an under-13 band, and a band's top can sit
  above 13 (call 1). An earlier draft quoted "the returned age range reflects regulatory
  requirements rather than the bounds of your age gates" here (not found on Apple's pages
  on 2026-11-19; unconfirmed).
- Repeat calls: "The API continues returning the previous range until the anniversary of
  their original declaration." Three more statements this note relied on were not found
  by the policy audit and are kept only as marked: "When in Ask First, by default, it will
  only prompt on the anniversary of the original response" (not found on Apple's pages on
  2026-11-19; unconfirmed); "Always shares silently; Never declines silently" (not found
  on Apple's pages on 2026-11-19; unconfirmed); and that Settings → [name] → Personal
  Information → Age Range for Apps → the app → "Share Age Range Again" forces a new answer
  sooner (not found on Apple's pages on 2026-11-19; unconfirmed). So whether a repeat ask
  shows Apple's sheet is not known from Apple's pages: the code makes no assumption about
  it (it asks, and takes what comes back), and the iPhone has to show it (below).
- Decline: expo throws `ERR_AGE_RANGE_USER_DECLINED`. Not available (no Apple Account, or
  not set up): `ERR_AGE_RANGE_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Before iOS 26 and on web expo returns a fake
  `lowerBound: 18`; the repo already refuses to call there.
- Other fields (`ageRangeDeclaration`, `activeParentalControls`) change nothing and are
  still not stored: an under-13 band is treated the same whoever declared it.
- `src/age/ageRange.test.ts` uses `(null, 13)` as its under-13 example; keep it and add
  `(null, 12)`.

## Build spec

As built, after the D70/D71 review's fixes.

**The one predicate:** `isUnder13Band` (pure, `src/engine/ageBand.ts`). **The one field:**
`useAgeStore.localOnly`, read through `isLocalOnly()`. **For automatic uploads at launch:**
`uploadsMayStart()` = `settled && !localOnly`.

### Core (types, db, engine, stores)

- `src/types/index.ts`: `export const LOCAL_ONLY_BELOW_AGE = 13;` and `AgeBucket`'s comment.
- `src/engine/ageBand.ts`: `isUnder13Band(bucket: AgeBucket | null | undefined): boolean`,
  call 1's predicate: an upper bound that is set, and a lower bound that is open or under
  13. No band is false. Malformed input counts as under 13 unless its lower bound reads 18
  or over. Never throws.
- `src/store/ageStore.ts` (a leaf: zustand, `@/db/ageBucket`, `@/engine/ageBand`,
  `@/types` only, so stores and services can both read it without a cycle):
  - state `{ bucket: AgeBucket | null; localOnly: boolean; settled: boolean }`, default
    `{ null, false, true }`;
  - `load(bucket, opts: { willAsk: boolean })`: sets the bucket, turns `localOnly` on for
    an under-13 band and never off, `settled = !willAsk`;
  - `loadUnknown(opts: { willAsk: boolean })`: the stored band couldn't be read. No band
    known, the gate closed for the run, `settled = !willAsk`, in one write;
  - `accept(bucket: AgeBucket): Promise<boolean>`: Apple's answer. An under-13 band closes
    the gate at once, before the save, and keeps it closed for the run even if the save
    fails. Any other band lifts the gate only once it is saved, and only if no later band
    came in meanwhile. True when the band was saved;
  - `markSettled()`;
  - `forgetBand(): Promise<boolean>`: bucket to null at once, `localOnly` left as it is
    for this run. From then on `accept` saves nothing and lifts nothing for the rest of
    the run, whenever the ask began (the re-review's round two): an answer from an ask
    that was in flight when Erase or account deletion ran never reaches storage, though
    an under-13 answer still closes the gate in memory. A save already running is undone
    once it lands, and the promise is false when that clear failed;
  - helpers `isLocalOnly()`, `uploadsMayStart()`, `whenAgeSettled(maxWaitMs?: number):
    Promise<void>` (resolves at once when settled, on `markSettled`, or at the cap; with
    no cap it waits for `markSettled` alone);
  - `LOCAL_ONLY_SIGN_IN` ("Accounts are off on this phone because of the age range it
    shares with Fairly Sure.") and `AGE_ANSWER_WAIT_MS` (5000) live here, in the leaf.
- `src/store/authStore.ts`: every sign-in and sign-up action goes through `accountSignIn`,
  which waits for Apple's answer at most 5 s on a launch that waits, then returns
  `LOCAL_ONLY_SIGN_IN` when local-only and `auth.AGE_PENDING_SIGN_IN` ("Sign-in isn't ready
  yet. Try again in a moment.") while the answer is still pending, and checks both again
  just before the service is called. Guest Erase and a confirmed account deletion call
  `forgetBand()` after the wipe. Sign-out is as built (its last push sends nothing under
  the rule, so "Sign out anyway?" names what is unsynced); when it fails after its wipe on
  a frozen account it returns `SIGN_OUT_FAILED_AFTER_WIPE_LOCAL_ONLY`. Delete account still
  calls the server.
- `src/store/ratingStore.ts`: nothing asked while Apple's answer is pending (the moment is
  kept), and no request unless `uploadsMayStart()` (on a local-only phone the moment is
  spent all the same).
- No SQLite migration: 023 stays the latest.
- Tests: the predicate's table (`src/engine/ageBand.test.ts`); the store's defaults, a
  lift on a 13+ band, an unknown band, `forgetBand` keeping `localOnly`, `whenAgeSettled`
  on `markSettled` and at its cap (`src/store/ageStore.test.ts`); an auth test that
  **enumerates** the store's `signIn*` / `signUp*` actions and asserts each refuses with a
  client whose every property access throws, so a new action is covered without editing
  the test (`src/store/authStore.localOnly.test.ts`); a stored session under the rule
  stays signed in and sends nothing; a band arriving mid-session wipes nothing;
  `deleteAccount` still reaches the server.

### Services

- `src/age/ageRange.ts`: `hydrateAgeGate(): Promise<void>` reads the stored band
  (`readStoredBand`: a band, none, or unknown when the row isn't a band or the read
  throws twice, a failed read being tried once more since round two; where Apple's API
  can never have run, `bandCouldBeStored`, a read that throws is none). Unknown closes the
  gate (`closeGateForUnknownBand` → `loadUnknown`); otherwise
  `load(band, { willAsk })`. `willAsk` is true when the API can run and the stored band is
  none, under 13 or unknown: only those launches leave the store unsettled, so only they
  make uploads wait. `ensureAgeBucket()` asks once per process wherever the API can run,
  whatever is stored (a band of 13 or over in the background, with the store already
  settled). A valid answer goes to `accept` (the band already stored, with the gate open,
  changes and writes nothing); a decline, "not available", a throw or a response that
  isn't a band keeps the stored band; it always ends in `markSettled()`.
  `recheckAgeBucketIfDue(now)` for the foreground, at most once per `AGE_RECHECK_MIN_MS`
  (24 h, in memory), while the rule applies or a band of 13 or over is known; not with no
  band, which the next launch asks about.
- `src/supabase/auth.ts`: `refuseWhenLocalOnly()` is the first statement of every exported
  sign-in and sign-up function. It waits for what is left of the 5 s since the age store
  began waiting, then returns `LOCAL_ONLY_SIGN_IN` when local-only and
  `AGE_PENDING_SIGN_IN` while the answer is still pending. `signInWithApple` asks it again
  before its sheet opens and once it closes, before the identity token reaches Supabase;
  `signInWithGoogle` the same, before the code is exchanged. `pauseSessionRefresh` and
  `resumeSessionRefresh` stop and start the session's refresh timer on a client that
  exists.
- `src/supabase/client.ts`: the client is made with `autoRefreshToken: uploadsMayStart()`,
  so off under the rule and off while Apple's answer is pending on a launch that waits.
- `src/supabase/sync.ts`: `syncNow`, `pushNow`, `backfillOffsets` do nothing when
  `isLocalOnly()`; the session check used between requests returns false too. On a launch
  that waits, a sweep waits at most 60 s (`AGE_SYNC_WAIT_MS`) and then goes on; `pushNow`
  at most 5 s. When the gate closes mid-sweep after a deletion's read-back restored a
  row, the stats and lists are rebuilt before the sweep stops (round two; a local
  recompute, no request).
- `src/analytics/track.ts`, `flush.ts`: nothing queued when local-only; nothing sent unless
  `uploadsMayStart()`. The flush's `maySend` re-checks that, the Usage stats switch and a
  guest's usage-notice stamp before it reads its queue and before every request, so a
  flush under way stops when any of them changes.
- `src/ai/coach.ts`, `refine.ts`: the empty result unless `uploadsMayStart()`, before a
  client, so under the rule and while Apple's answer is pending (round two; `isLocalOnly()`
  alone before). `src/notifications/trialReminder.ts`: its RevenueCat read waits the same
  way, and a reminder already scheduled is left as it is meanwhile.
- `src/billing/revenuecat.ts`: `apiKeyForPlatform()` returns `''` when `isLocalOnly()`
  (every function treats that as unavailable, and checks the key before it loads the
  SDK). **A stale account is dropped before anything is read** (round two,
  `dropCachedAccount`, `identity`): since D70 a sign-out can happen while RevenueCat isn't
  running (a frozen account, or Apple's answer pending), so RevenueCat never hears of it
  and its SDK still holds that account. When RevenueCat then starts with nobody signed
  in, the app asks the SDK whether its user is anonymous, logs out if it isn't, and only
  then reads entitlements, so the old account's Plus can't appear on the guest and a
  purchase can't land on the old account. If the SDK can't say, entitlement reads report
  "couldn't find out" (the mirror is left as it is), plans don't load, and purchases and
  restore report "unavailable", until a sign-in or the next launch. `src/billing/init.ts`: `startBilling` configures RevenueCat only once
  `uploadsMayStart()`, so never under the rule and never while the answer is pending, with
  no cap; once on a lift. `ensureBillingStarted(maxWaitMs = 5000)` for the paywall waits
  for the answer at most 5 s and, with it still pending, starts nothing and resolves
  false. A sign-out before RevenueCat has started sets the Plus mirror to free, a local
  write.
- `src/age/localOnly.ts`: `initLocalOnly()`. On entering the rule: clear the queued
  events, forget the usage-notice stamp (once the settings have been read), stop the
  session's refresh timer, reset the Coach. On leaving: sync if signed in. Once uploads
  may start (settled and not local-only, after a lift or after Apple's answer on a launch
  that waited): resume the session's refresh, flush if allowed, start billing.
- `src/supabase/account.ts`: no gate; a comment naming it as the permitted exception.
- Tests: `ensureAgeBucket` asks once per process whatever is stored; a 13+ answer lifts; a
  decline, a throw or an invalid band keeps the stored band; an unknown stored band closes
  the gate; web, Android and iOS 18 are settled at once (`src/age/ageRange.test.ts`). A
  pin on `expo-age-range`'s response shape (`src/age/ageRangeResponse.test.ts`, round
  two): types that stop `tsc`, the first step of `check.sh`, if an upgrade moves or
  renames `lowerBound` or `upperBound`, and a read of the package's native iOS record for
  the same two fields. Without it such an upgrade would read every answer as no band,
  and the rule would silently stop applying. A
  stored adult band asked about in the background, with an under-13 answer stopping a
  flush and a sweep in flight (`src/age/backgroundAsk.test.ts`). One test per gated module
  with the throwing client. Billing: `configure` never called when local-only or while the
  answer is pending, called after `markSettled` otherwise
  (`src/billing/localOnly.test.ts`). The installed auth-js's renewal of an expired stored
  session, pinned (`src/supabase/auth.localOnly.test.ts`). **The guard,
  `src/age/egress.test.ts`:** it reads every non-test `.ts` and `.tsx` under `src/` and
  `app/` with the TypeScript parser, so comments and strings don't count, and checks that
  (1) the files that can send over the network equal a pinned list; (2) in each, the count
  of each kind of network call equals a pinned number, so a new call in a listed file
  fails until someone has looked at it (`.from(`, `.functions` and `peekSupabaseClient`
  count too); (3) outside the list, nothing imports the Supabase client module's values
  but `isSupabaseConfigured`, under any alias, by import, require, `import()` or
  re-export; (4) each listed file but `account.ts` reads the age store; (5) every entry
  point that can reach a request checks the rule before the first request, exported
  functions and the methods of objects and classes alike; (6) the entry points this note
  names check it in their own body; (7) every exported `sign(In|Up)…` function in
  `auth.ts` opens with exactly `const refused = await refuseWhenLocalOnly();` and `if
  (refused) return refused;`, read from the syntax tree. A new upload file, a new call in
  a listed one or a new sign-in route fails the test until it is listed and gated. What it
  can't see is in its own header (a client reached through a parameter in an unlisted file
  under method names that aren't markers; a gate whose result is computed and ignored).

## For counsel (not settled here)

Revised 2026-11-19 after the policy audit of this rule (three points widened, three added).

1. Whether Apple's declared band is COPPA "actual knowledge" (the rule assumes yes), and
   California's AB 1043 (from 2027-01-01), which secondary sources say treats a developer
   that receives the signal as having actual knowledge.
2. **First:** what must happen to everything already on a server or a processor once a
   phone shows an under-13 band, and how fast: the frozen account's rows, its usage
   events, its session's IP address, its Coach request count and any RevenueCat record
   made before the band. The FTC's COPPA FAQ (H.6, A.12) says an operator that learns a
   user is a child "must either meet COPPA's notice and parental consent requirements or
   delete the child's information". Today the app keeps them until the person taps Delete
   account, which stays available; the band never reaches the server, so any deletion has
   to start on the phone. Also here: a frozen account's sign-in is renewed each time the
   app starts (the refresh token and the request's IP address go to the hosting provider).
3. Whether asking once and carrying on after a decline meets the state acts' "verify".
4. The significant-change notices and consent-revocation handling the acts and Apple
   describe; the app has neither. Apple's server notification for revoked consent
   (`RESCIND_CONSENT`) may go to RevenueCat's address, not the owner's: check where App
   Store server notifications are sent.
5. Texas's delete-after-verification clause against a band kept on the phone.
6. Every duty Texas sets for ages 13 to 17, the contract clause for minors' subscriptions
   among them.
7. Quebec's consent rule for under-14s (s. 4.1 of its private-sector privacy act): with
   Apple's gates at 13, 16 and 18, a 13-year-old there isn't caught by this rule. Under-16
   consent ages elsewhere wait for an EU or UK launch.
8. Bands from regions whose gates don't include 13, such as (none, 15): Apple says the
   gates a region uses "may differ from the age gates you specify". Since the review the
   rule treats any band that can include someone under 13 as under 13 (call 1), which
   makes 13-to-15-year-olds there local-only too. Is that the right side to err on?
9. Whether the links to the privacy and support pages, which open in Safari on a
   local-only phone (GitHub logs the visitor's IP address), need anything.

## What only the iPhone can show

- Whether under 13 comes back as 12 or 13 (the predicate takes both). Sandbox: Developer →
  Sandbox Apple Account → Manage → Age Assurance → "Under 13", Erase, relaunch; needs
  Developer Mode, a Sandbox Apple Account and iOS 26.2 or later.
- Whether a repeat ask is silent for a stored adult band: since the review every launch
  asks, so an adult who shared a band of 13 or over should see no sheet at the second
  launch and nothing should wait. Apple's pages confirm only that "the API continues
  returning the previous range until the anniversary of their original declaration".
- Whether a decline is asked again at each launch, and whether that shows the sheet each
  time.
- Whether a request can stay unresolved. It leaves billing, the guest flush, the rating
  prompt and the Coach waiting for that run, and since the review it also blocks sign-in and plans for
  that run: sign-in is refused with "Sign-in isn't ready yet. Try again in a moment." and
  the paywall shows no plans. The next launch asks again.
- What the paywall does when it is opened while Apple's sheet is up: after 5 s it should
  show "Plus plans couldn't load. Check your connection, then open this again.", and load
  the plans once the answer is in. The plans are then asked of a RevenueCat SDK that
  hasn't been configured (`fetchPlans` in `src/billing/revenuecat.ts` checks the key, not
  whether the SDK was configured), which is expected to fail without sending anything; no
  device has shown it.
- `expo-age-range` 0.2.21 asserts on an unknown declaration value (`confirmed` exists from
  iOS 26.5); a debug development build may trap there.

Answered since, without the iPhone: whether supabase-js refreshes a token inside
`getSession()` after auto-refresh is stopped. It does. Against the installed auth-js
2.106.2 (`src/supabase/auth.localOnly.test.ts`), a stored session that has already expired
is renewed when the client is made and at `getSession()`, whatever `autoRefreshToken`
says. The first two residuals below follow from it.

## Residuals

What the rule still lets through, after the D70/D71 review. Each is disclosed in the held
privacy policy (§2, §9) and `docs/APP_PRIVACY.md`; the refresh cases are on counsel's list
(point 2).

1. **A frozen account's sign-in is renewed each time the app starts with it expired**: the
   refresh token and the request's IP address go to the hosting provider, which records the
   address with the session; no predictions. Not "once", as the first draft said.
2. **On a launch that waits for Apple's answer, a signed-in account's expired session can
   be renewed before the answer**, for the same reason.
3. **After a background under-13 answer on a phone where uploads had started, a request
   already in flight completes**, and **RevenueCat stays configured for that run**. The app
   asks it for nothing more, and from the next launch it is never configured. Once
   RevenueCat's SDK is configured it keeps refreshing customer information on its own for
   the rest of that run, and it can't be unconfigured.
4. **Google sign-in:** the rule is checked again when Google's sheet closes, before the
   code is exchanged, but Supabase has already created or found the user at its own
   callback inside the browser by then. The app refuses the session and the code expires
   unused; the server-side user exists.
5. **Email sign-in:** one sent before a background under-13 answer goes through, and the
   account is then frozen.
6. **An adult whose request to Apple never resolves** on a launch that waits gets no
   plans, purchase, restore or sign-in for that run, and since the re-review's round two
   no Coach answer either. The next launch is fine if the request resolves then.
7. **The "couldn't find out" hold on billing** (the re-review's round two). When RevenueCat
   starts with nobody signed in, the app logs out any account its SDK still holds from a
   sign-out RevenueCat never saw, before reading an entitlement. If the SDK can't say
   whether its user is anonymous, even after that log-out, entitlement reads report
   "couldn't find out", plans don't load, and purchases and restore are "unavailable",
   until a sign-in or the next launch. So a guest on such a phone can't buy or restore
   for that run. Chosen over the other way to be wrong: an earlier account's Plus showing
   on the guest, or a purchase landing on that account. Whether a fresh install's SDK
   always answers is for the iPhone to show (`HUMAN_VERIFICATION.md` D5).
