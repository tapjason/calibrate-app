# Day 0 → Day 1: research behind D18 and the reminder ask

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: the claims the rebuilt Day 0 now makes, and what
decides whether someone comes back the next evening. Companion to
[`retention-2026-10.md`](retention-2026-10.md) and
[`confidence-2026-10.md`](confidence-2026-10.md).

## 1. The planning-fallacy line is accurate

The Warmup verdict says "students who expected to finish their thesis in 34 days took
56." Checked against the paper (Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, *JPSP*, [PDF](https://bear.warrington.ufl.edu/brenner/mar7588/Papers/buehler-et-al-1994.pdf);
[summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_fallacy)): 37 psychology honours
students predicted 33.9 days on average and took 55.5; about 30% (29.7%) finished by
their own predicted date; their *worst-case* estimate averaged 48.6 days and fewer than
half beat even that. Estimates for other people were less biased than for themselves.
So the copy is right, the "about 30% on time" figure is available if a line ever needs
a second number, and the finding is about own plans, which is the argument for D18.

## 2. When to ask for notification permission

No controlled study turned up; the material is vendor guidance (OneSignal, Plotline,
BuildFire citing Airship 2023 and Braze 2022, others) and it agrees on direction:

- Asking on first launch is the biggest cause of low opt-in; ask after a value moment
  (a first save, a finished first session).
- A primer screen before the system dialog is recommended, and lets someone say "not
  now" without spending the one native iOS prompt. The headline multipliers (2-3×,
  25% → 50%) are unsourced; treat as direction only.
- iOS shows the system alert once; after a decline only Settings can change it.
- Vendor iOS opt-in benchmarks run roughly 40–65%.

**What Calibrate does:** exactly the recommended pattern. `ReminderPrompt` shows a primer
card on Today only once there is a prediction to be reminded about, names the date of
the first reminder, and shows the system alert only from "Turn on reminders"
(roadmap step 38).

## 3. The gap this found: "Not now" costs the Day-1 reminder

D18 makes the first prediction due tomorrow, so its 19:00 reminder is the entire Day-1
hook. But "Not now" holds the card back `REMINDER_PROMPT_COOLDOWN_DAYS = 7`
(`ReminderPrompt.tsx`), so someone who declines on Day 0, perhaps before seeing why,
is not asked again until after the prediction is due. Nothing then brings them back
on Day 1 except opening the app themselves.

**Proposal (D19, not built):** when the earliest open prediction is due within two
days, "Not now" holds the card until that day's morning instead of 7 days, once; a
second "Not now" uses the 7-day cooldown. The card's copy already says what it is
for, and this is the only moment the ask has a deadline. Measure opt-in and Day-1
return before and after (D18's metrics). If a decision isn't wanted, the cost is
small and known: some Day-0 decliners miss their first reminder.

## 4. Day-1 benchmarks are weak evidence

Adjust 2024 (via vendor summaries): mean mobile D1 ≈ 25%, D30 ≈ 6%, D90 < 4%; finance
apps ≈ 34%. Other quoted ranges (40–60% for strong onboarding, 15–25% for broken) are
unsourced. One edtech case study moved D1 from 20% to 35% by aligning acquisition and
onboarding (before/after, no control). Definitions differ (classic vs rolling
retention); compare like with like. **Use:** nothing here to build against; D18's own
before/after (`warmup_completed`, first log, D1) is the measurement that matters.

[OneSignal](https://documentation.onesignal.com/docs/en/prompt-for-push-permissions),
