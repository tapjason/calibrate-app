# Round 12 plan, 2026-11-12: UI stress

The plan for the twelfth blind round (`/playtest stress`). The findings go in
`playtest-2026-11-12.md`. Where this differs from `CLAUDE.md`,
`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` or `UI_ROADMAP.md`, those win.

## Focus

The owner's report from the iPhone 17 (2026-11-12): Log's title row and its ×
"sometimes" sat under the Dynamic Island, so leaving a new prediction was hard
(fixed the same day, `1851d9d`). The owner asked for "even more UI stressing
playtests". So this round pushes the edges: the biggest and smallest phones,
large text and high contrast, crowded lists, long and non-Latin
text, four-digit counts, impatient tapping, a slow phone offline, and
non-touch input (stylus, Voice Control). It is the first round on the
playtest profiles (`src/dev/playtestProfiles.ts`, app-profile §9) and the
first with the ten personas added the same day.

The cast was first written with Lin in landscape; the app is portrait only on
iOS (`app.json`), so her run moved to portrait before it started, and the stress
pass's landscape shots count as web-only.

Before the blind runs, `web-playtester` runs the stress pass in the playtest
skill (every screen and sheet at four sizes, both appearances, zoom 1 and 2;
the impatient checks; time zones, midnight, CPU and offline).

## Common blocks (in every brief)

**A. What you know** (the App Store page, word for word; nothing else):

> **Fairly Sure** · Know when to trust your gut · Productivity · Free, offers in-app purchases
>
> Log a prediction, say how sure you are, and find out if you were right to be. Sharp in
> health, shaky with money? Your calibration score shows you where.
>
> Most people are overconfident about some things and underconfident about others, and
> never find out which. Fairly Sure shows you.
>
> HOW IT WORKS. Write a prediction ("I'll finish the report by Friday"), set how confident
> you are, from 0 to 100%, and pick a due date. As you set it, you see how your past calls
> at that confidence turned out. When it comes due, Fairly Sure reminds you. Tap yes or
> no. That's it.
>
> YOUR RESULT IN 60 SECONDS. Don't want to wait weeks? The warmup quiz gives you a first
> read on day one: ten quick either-or questions, each with how sure you are, and how
> your confidence compared with how many you got right.

**B. The edges.** This round is about whether the app holds up for how you use
your phone. Whenever something is cut off, overlaps, is hard to reach or
close, moves under your finger, or takes more than a moment, write "Edge: <what,
where>" with a screenshot, and keep going. Say what you expected before each
meaningful tap and whether that happened. Rate each task's ease 1–7 and each
issue's severity 0–4.

**C. Rules.** http://localhost:8099. Never sign in, never buy, never erase
data, stay on localhost. Screenshots
`.playwright-mcp/playtest-2026-11-12/run<n>-<moment>.png`. Answer the three
end questions from memory, without going back to look.

## The briefs

### Run 1 · Olu, 39 · fresh install · 440 × 956 light · one thumb

**The person.** Olu dispatches delivery drivers in Houston. He's on a crowded
bus, standing, an iPhone Pro Max in his right hand, thumb only: anything in the
top third of the screen means shifting his grip, which he hates and sometimes
can't do. He taps fast and doesn't read much. A coworker said this app tells
you whether your gut is any good; his gut runs his job ("that driver will be
late", "this route clears by 4"). Native English, everyday tech. Fair but
impatient. Treat any control in the top 200 px as a stretch: say so each time
you have to use one, and prefer anything lower. Use 440 × 956, light.

**Tasks, in his words:**
1. See what this thing says about your gut, fast.
2. Put in tomorrow's guess about a driver running late.
3. You started another one by mistake: get out of it without saving.
4. Make sure the app tells you when tomorrow's guess is due.
5. Change your mind about how sure you were on the driver one, if it lets you.
6. Find where the app shows how sure you usually are versus how often you're right.

**Interruption.** Halfway through typing your second prediction, the bus
brakes: reload the page, then carry on.

**End questions.** 1. What will the app do tomorrow, and what do you do then?
2. What did the quiz say about your gut, and how much should you trust it?
3. Why couldn't (or could) you change how sure you were?

### Run 2 · Inès, 33 · six days in · 402 × 874 dark · impatient

**The person.** Inès is a triage nurse in Marseille. She uses her phone in

back later expecting her work to still be there. She's used the app for six

due today. French first, fluent English, but she skims. Judge hard: lost work
or doubled entries make her quit. Use 402 × 874, dark. Double-click buttons
that save or answer, as she would.

**Tasks, in her words:**
1. Deal with whatever is waiting for you today.
2. Add "my 10 km on Sunday is under 55 minutes", quickly.
3. Start another one, get called away (go back or close it), and come back
   expecting it to still be there.
4. Check you didn't accidentally add anything twice.
5. You answered something wrongly by tapping too fast: fix it.
6. Find what the app says about your sport predictions so far.

**Interruption.** In task 3, close whatever you have open the way you would on
a phone (back, or the close control) without saving.

**End questions.** 1. What happens if you tap Save twice? 2. Why does one of
your answers not count (if one doesn't)? 3. How long until the app tells you
something about yourself?

## Success criteria (for synthesis only, never in a brief)

   thumb; leaving a typed Log asks first on iOS (web drops it, web-only);
   reminders: You → Due-day reminders; the confidence never editable (the
   record); Insights' chart found. Every top-200 px control he had to use is
   counted.
2. **Inès:** a double Save makes one prediction; a closed Log loses its draft
   (iOS asks first); Change answer within 24 hours; the same-day "not scored"
   line read rightly; nothing doubled in History.
3. **Lin:** every control she needs works by tapping alone; the ±5 steppers set a
   confidence without a drag; the answer unlocks the rating and health together
   and says so; the score's give-or-take understood; Edit changes the due date.
4. **Viktor:** Answer all walks the overdue pile; long titles readable in full
   (details or Resolve); the betting category read as his own, never
   encouraged as betting advice; truncated category names distinguishable;
   Categories hides or renames without losing predictions; Change answer.
5. **Mei:** every control she needs has a name matching its words; the chart's
   ranges read through their labels; Share reachable; the reminder time in You;
   Change answer.
6. **Tunde:** nothing fails offline (the Paywall says it can't load);
   Welcome back names the gap and what's waiting; Answer all; the streak's end
   said without blame; the app usable at 4× CPU.
7. **Gloria:** the 1,001-day streak readable at zoom 1.5; the answer recorded;

8. **Aisling:** Answer all for two due today; Log it again for tomorrow; Change
